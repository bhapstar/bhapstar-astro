Focus does not stay where you put it

Telescope tubes expand and contract as the temperature falls through the night, and the optical path changes with them. A tube in focus at nine in the evening is not in focus at two in the morning.

How much that matters depends on the focal ratio, and it matters more than most people expect. The usable focus zone scales with the square of the f-number, so an f/5 instrument tolerates roughly a quarter the error of an f/10 one. At the fast end you are working within a few tens of microns, comfortably less than a night's thermal drift.

What it does about it

The EAF replaces the focuser knob with a stepper motor driven from the ASIAIR. It runs an autofocus routine, stepping through positions and measuring star sizes to find best focus without anyone touching the telescope, and it refocuses during the session, either at intervals or when the temperature has moved by a set amount.

The second is the one that changes results. Refocusing periodically keeps the whole night's subs usable instead of accumulating a drift that quietly ruins the last third of them. It also removes a source of vibration, since focusing by hand means touching the telescope and waiting for it to settle, several times a night.

Fitting and pairing

Installation means removing a focuser knob and fitting the motor bracket, so it is worth confirming the bracket suits your focuser before ordering, as ZWO supply different fittings. Checking and configuring backlash after installation makes the autofocus routine noticeably better behaved.

It pairs naturally with the ASI585MC Air, which provides the ASIAIR side of the arrangement, and it matters most on the faster Askar V configurations. For short sessions on slow optics it is a convenience rather than a necessity.

Mine came from AliExpress and arrived in Dubai without trouble. Since the bracket has to match your focuser, this is one to confirm before ordering rather than after, because returning a small mechanical part internationally costs more in effort than the part is worth.
