Why an imager keeps eyepieces

An imaging rig never shows you the sky. It shows you a screen. That is fine for producing photographs and slightly beside the point on a clear night when someone asks what you are looking at.

This kit stays in the bag for two reasons. Putting an eyepiece in and finding something by eye is sometimes quicker than waiting on a plate solve, particularly on the Moon and the planets. And handing someone a telescope they can actually look through is a completely different experience from showing them a stacked image afterwards.

What a kit like this is good at

Covering a range of magnifications at once, without buying premium eyepieces one at a time. For occasional visual use alongside an imaging setup that is the right trade. The colour and neutral density filters earn their place on the Moon, which is bright enough at low magnification to be genuinely uncomfortable.

Bundled eyepieces are rarely the equal of good individual ones, so expect narrower apparent fields and less generous eye relief than a dedicated wide-field eyepiece would give. If visual observing were my main interest I would buy two good eyepieces instead of a kit of eight, but that is a different hobby from this one.

Pairing

The 2 inch fitting suits the Askar V, and the longer configurations give the most comfortable visual magnifications.
