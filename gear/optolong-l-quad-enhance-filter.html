The filter for everything narrowband cannot touch

The L-Quad Enhance is the other half of that pair. Rather than opening two narrow windows, it keeps most of the spectrumThe full spread of colours light can be, from deep blue through to deep red and beyond at both ends. and removes the slice where sodium and mercury lighting sits.

This is the opposite approach to a narrowband filter, and worth being clear about because the "quad bandpass" name misleads people. It does not open four narrow slots. It keeps almost the whole spectrum and removes one slice, roughly 575 to 625nm, where sodium and mercury lighting sits. The four bands in the name are the emission lines that happen to fall in the kept regions, at better than 90% transmission each. Keeping the continuumLight spread smoothly across every colour, which is how stars and galaxies shine. The interesting part

Optolong rate this filter for Bortle 1 to 7, and explicitly say Bortle 8 and 9 are not recommended, pointing you at their 3nm and 7nm narrowband filters instead. My balcony in Dubai is Bortle 8, so on the manufacturer's own advice this filter should be the wrong choice there.

That makes it worth testing properly rather than assuming. Broadband filters work by blocking what the local lighting emits, and a city running white LED puts out light across exactly the range you want to keep, while older sodium lighting produces a narrow spike that is easy to reject. A filter of this kind can therefore be effective in one city and marginal in another, which is why a glowing review written elsewhere tells you very little.

Where it earns its place

The darker sites are the obvious case. At Al Qudra and Al Quaa, where the sky is doing far less damage to begin with, broadband targets are the ones worth pointing at. M81 and M82 and the Needle Galaxy both came through this filter from Al Qudra.

The one that surprises me is Orion, which is an emission nebula and by the usual logic belongs to the L-eXtreme. M42 is bright enough, and has enough going on beyond the two narrowband lines, that keeping the continuum was the better call.

For fainter emission nebulae the L-eXtreme is still the right call. Expect a gain in contrast and easier colour calibration from this one rather than a transformation, since there is far less a broadband filter can safely block.
