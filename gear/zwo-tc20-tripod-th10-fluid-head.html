The part nobody thinks about

A tripod is the last thing people consider and often the first thing that limits them. One that flexes turns every gust into trailed stars, and no amount of guidingA second small camera watches one star and sends tiny corrections to the mount, keeping the target locked in place over long exposures. rescues a support that is moving. That alone would justify a page.

The TC20 and TH10 work as a pair. The tripod provides a stable base, the head provides smooth damped movement on top of it. But filing the TH10 under "accessory" undersells it badly, because on a Seestar it is the part that unlocks equatorial modeTilting an ordinary up-down, left-right telescope so one axis lines up with Earth's axis. The TH10 is what makes that tilt practical rather than a fight. It has a graduated tilt scale, a bubble level, and fine geared adjustment that is damped rather than free, so a small correction holds where you put it instead of drifting back under the weight of the telescope. Any 3/8-16 head will bolt a Seestar on. Very few will let you dial in a latitude angle and trust it to stay there for three hours.

Smooth continuous motion. In practice this also carries a camera for wide-field and star-trail work, and a small refractorA telescope that uses lenses to gather light, like a very good spotting scope. None of that makes it an equatorial mount. The TH10 has no motors and does not track anything. It is a wedge with a damping mechanism: it sets the angle and holds it, and the Seestar does the tracking from there. Put a camera or the Askar V on top instead and the tracking has to come from somewhere else, which is what the harmonic mount is for.

Judging a tripod

Two things decide whether one is genuinely stable: how quickly vibration damps out after you touch it, and whether the leg locks hold under load without creeping. Both are easy to test at home with the payloadThe weight a mount is built to carry. Loading one near its limit works, but everything about it behaves worse than it does at half. mounted, and both tell you more than a load rating does.

The carbon legs matter for a duller reason than load capacity. A Seestar is light, and light instruments are the ones that move when a breeze comes through or when someone walks past on a balcony. Stiffness here is not about carrying weight, it is about not passing vibration into a lens that is midway through a 60 second sub.
