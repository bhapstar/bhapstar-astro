Askar V Modular APO Refractor

The Askar V is a unique telescope system that features two interchangeable 60mm and 80mm ED apochromatic objectives, a reducer, a flattener and an extender. This offers six distinct focal lengths from 270mm to 600mm. Wide and sweeping one night, tight and intimate the next.

One telescope, six framings

Focal lengthHow much a lens or telescope magnifies, measured in millimetres. A small number gives a wide view of the sky, a large number a narrow, closer one. decides framing, and no single focal length frames everything. The usual answer is to own several telescopes. The Askar V answers it the other way: one tube, interchangeable 60mm and 80mm objectivesThe main lens at the front of a refractor. How it gets used

Changing configuration is a bench job rather than a field job, so in practice the target is chosen before going out and the scope is set up for it. That suits the way I work anyway, particularly for a drive out to darker skies where the night is planned in advance.

Most of the deep-skyAnything beyond our own solar system: nebulae, galaxies and star clusters. Faint, far away, and the reason for long exposures. gallery came through this telescope, across most of its range. The Rosette and the Christmas Tree at 60mm with the reducer, the Cat's Paw at 80mm native, and Thor's Helmet at 80mm with the extender, which is the tightest the system goes.

Worth knowing that the 60mm and 80mm objectives differ in apertureThe size of the opening the light comes through, written as an f number such as f/2.8. Confusingly, a smaller f number means a bigger opening and more light. as well as focal length, so the widest configurations gather less light than the longest ones. Six focal lengths is closer in practice to two telescopes with useful adjustment either side, which is still a great deal more than one.

What it pairs with

It sits on the Juwei 14 without difficulty, and the ASI585MC Air's small sensor is a sensible match across most of the range. The EAF is close to essential at the faster end, where the usable focus zone is genuinely small.

The supplied case holds everything properly, which matters when the gear travels. It is bulky for hand luggage, but it means nothing gets left behind.

How mine got to the UAE

Bought from High Point Scientific in the United States and forwarded to Dubai through MyUS. Neither end gave any trouble, which is worth saying because the usual obstacle to buying astronomy gear from this part of the world is not price, it is whether anyone will ship at all.

If package forwarding is new to you, the arrangement is simple: the forwarder gives you a US address, the retailer ships domestically to it, and the forwarder reships onward. It turns any US shop that will not post internationally into one that effectively does.

The timings, since nobody publishes these. High Point despatched within a couple of days. MyUS took about a week to process the package once it arrived, which is the step people underestimate. Onward shipping was another week or so. Around two and a half weeks door to door, and the forwarding and import charges came to roughly 250 US dollars on top of the purchase price.

That figure is the one to plan around. On a telescope at the old price it is a meaningful addition, and on a cheaper item it can be most of what you pay. Consolidating several purchases into one forwarded shipment is the obvious way to soften it.
