ZWO ASI585MC Air Camera

A cooled, guided, controlled imaging system compressed into a single body. The Sony IMX585 sensor at its heart is extraordinarily sensitive to faint light, and the built-in cooling pulls heat from the chip to keep the long exposures clean and quiet. Guide camera, controller, and wireless connection are all folded in, leaving nothing between the telescope and the sky but the lens cap, before it comes off.

Three boxes in one

A conventional imaging setup needs a main camera, a guide camera with its own optics, and a control computer, each with cables and power of its own. The ASI585MC Air puts the imaging sensor, a 2.1MP guide sensor and a full ASIAIR controller inside one housing.

What that means in the field is one power lead and a phone. Anyone who has lost a session to an intermittent USB fault in the dark will recognise why that matters more than any figure on the box.

The sensor

The IMX585 is an 8.3 megapixel back-illuminated STARVIS 2 sensor, 3840 by 2160, with 2.9 micron pixels on a 1/1.2 inch format and peak quantum efficiency around 88%. Small pixels suit short and medium focal lengths, which makes it a natural partner for the Askar V across most of its range.

Cooling is a two-stage thermoelectric system rated to hold the sensor 30 to 35 degrees below ambient. In a temperate climate that is a convenience. Imaging through a Gulf summer it is the feature that makes long exposures viable at all, since dark current roughly doubles for every 6 to 7 degrees the sensor gains.

Framing, and what the format means

The 1/1.2 inch format gives a narrower field than an APS-C camera on the same telescope, so the largest targets want a shorter focal length or a mosaic. In exchange, a small sensor asks much less of the optics at the edges and makes flats considerably less troublesome. Most of the recent gallery was shot with it, including Orion and the Flame and Horsehead.

It works well with the filter drawer and the EAF, which between them mean a filter change or a refocus does not involve touching the imaging train. One consequence of the all-in-one design is that the parts are married, so moving to a larger sensor later means replacing the controller and guide camera too.

Getting one to the UAE

Mine came directly from ZWO in China and arrived in Dubai without any difficulty. Buying straight from the manufacturer is the shortest route for ZWO kit, and for a camera with an integrated controller it has the additional advantage that warranty and firmware support come from the same place that built it.
