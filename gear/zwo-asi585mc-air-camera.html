ZWO ASI585MC Air Camera

A cooled, guided, controlled imaging system compressed into a single body. The Sony IMX585 sensor at its heart is extraordinarily sensitive to faint light, and the built-in cooling pulls heat from the chip to keep the long exposures clean and quiet. Guide camera, controller, and wireless connection are all folded in, leaving nothing between the telescope and the sky but the lens cap, before it comes off.

Three boxes become one

A conventional imaging setup needs a main camera, a guide camera with its own scope or off-axis guider, and a control computer, each with its own cables and its own power. The ASI585MC Air puts the imaging sensor, a guide sensor and a full ASIAIR controller in one housing.

The result is one power lead and a wireless connection to a phone. Anyone who has spent a session chasing an intermittent USB fault in the dark will understand why that matters more than any specification on the box.

The sensor

The IMX585 is an 8.3 megapixel back-illuminated sensor, 3840 by 2160, with 2.9 micron pixels on a 1/1.2 inch format. Small pixels suit short and medium focal lengths, which is what makes it a sensible partner for a scope in the 400mm to 600mm range.

Cooling is a two-stage thermoelectric system rated to hold the sensor 30 to 35 degrees below ambient. In a cool climate that is a convenience. Imaging from Dubai it is closer to essential, because dark current roughly doubles for every 6 to 7 degrees of sensor temperature, and summer ambient here does not cooperate.

The trade-off nobody mentions

A small sensor is not automatically a disadvantage, but it is a constraint. The 1/1.2 inch format gives a narrower field than an APS-C camera on the same telescope, so framing large targets means either a shorter focal length or a mosaic. Set against that, small sensors are less demanding of the optics at the edges, and there is less to go wrong with flats.

The all-in-one design also means the components are married. If you later want a larger sensor, you are replacing the controller and guide camera too.

Who it suits

People who value getting started quickly over maximum configurability, and anyone imaging from a location where the setup has to be carried out and packed away every session. If you have a permanent setup and already own an ASIAIR, you are paying twice for the same controller.
