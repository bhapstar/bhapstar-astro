ZWO Seestar S30 Pro Smart Telescope

Keeps the S30's 30mm aperture but moves to an 8.3MP IMX585 behind a 160mm f/5.3 quadruplet, and adds a wide-angle camera for Milky Way and star-trail work. More resolution and more coverage, not more light.

Same aperture, different instrument

The headline number has not moved. Both the S30 and the S30 Pro have a 30mm aperture, so the Pro collects no more light than the original. Anyone expecting to reach fainter targets because of the name will be disappointed.

Everything behind the aperture has changed. The Pro uses an 8.3 megapixel IMX585 in place of the S30's 2.1 megapixel IMX662, on a 1/1.2 inch format rather than 1/2.8 inch, behind a four-element apochromat at 160mm f/5.3 instead of the triplet at 150mm f/5. A second wide-angle camera is what makes the Milky Way and star-trail modes possible.

What four times the pixels actually buys

Less than it sounds, and more than you would think. The larger sensor is paired with a proportionally longer focal length, so the angular size of a pixel barely changes: the Pro works out around 3.7 arcseconds per pixel, coarse either way.

What you gain is coverage. The Pro's telephoto field is roughly 2.2 by 4 degrees, which fits targets in one frame that the S30 has to crop or mosaic. The wide-angle camera is a separate matter again: this Milky Way frame from Al Quaa is its work, and is not something the original S30 could attempt at all.

The rest of the changes

Battery life roughly doubles, from about three and a half hours to around six, which on a full night is the difference between one session and two. Storage goes from 64GB to 128GB, which matters because 4K files are much larger. There is a built-in dew heater and NFC pairing. Processing after a session is slower rather than faster, around a minute against fifteen seconds, simply because there is four times as much data to denoise.

The Milky Way and star-trail modes are genuine features that want a dark sky to show what they can do, so they belong on a trip to Al Quaa rather than a balcony night.

Which to buy

For more coverage, better colour correction and longer sessions, the Pro is the better instrument and the price difference is defensible. If the goal is reaching faint objects, neither helps, because the limit is the 30mm aperture, and the S50 with its 50mm aperture is the more logical purchase at the cost of a much tighter field. The S30 has now been discontinued, which settles the question for most people.

Getting one to the UAE

Mine came directly from ZWO in China and reached Dubai without any difficulty, which has been true of everything I have ordered from them. For a sealed instrument that receives regular firmware updates, buying from the manufacturer also means support comes from the people who wrote the software.
