Same aperture, different instrument

The headline number has not moved. Both the S30 and the S30 Pro have a 30mm aperture, so the Pro collects no more light than the original. Anyone expecting to reach fainter targets because of the name is going to be disappointed.

What changed is everything behind the aperture. The Pro uses an 8.3 megapixel IMX585 in place of the S30's 2.1 megapixel IMX662, on a 1/1.2 inch format rather than 1/2.8 inch, and puts it behind a four-element apochromat at 160mm f/5.3 instead of the S30's triplet at 150mm f/5. It also adds a second wide-angle camera, which is what makes the Milky Way and star-trail modes possible.

The resolution question, honestly

Four times the pixels sounds decisive. It is more subtle than that. The larger sensor is paired with a proportionally longer focal length, so the angular size of a pixel barely changes between the two models. The Pro's image scale works out around 3.7 arcseconds per pixel, which is coarse either way.

What you actually gain is coverage. The Pro's telephoto field is roughly 2.2 by 4 degrees, which fits large targets in one frame that the S30 has to crop or mosaic. That is a real, visible difference on the Andromeda Galaxy, the Pleiades and the Orion complex.

What is genuinely better

Battery life roughly doubles, from around three and a half hours to about six. On a full night that is the difference between one session and two. Storage goes from 64GB to 128GB, which matters because 4K files are much larger. The built-in dew heater is welcome, and NFC pairing removes a small annoyance.

What is worse

Post-session processing is slower, not faster. The AI denoising step runs around a minute on the Pro against roughly fifteen seconds on the S30, simply because there is four times as much data. Over an evening of short captures that adds up.

The Milky Way and star-trail modes are real features, but they are wide-field modes that depend on a dark sky. Reviewers working in light-polluted suburbs have consistently found them underwhelming, and ZWO's own promotional comparison was shot in urban conditions and acknowledged the limitation.

Which one to buy

If you want more coverage, better colour correction and longer sessions, the Pro is the better instrument and the price difference is defensible. If your priority is reaching faint objects, neither model helps, because the limit is the 30mm aperture. In that case the S50 with its 50mm aperture is the more logical purchase, at the cost of a much tighter field.

There is a reasonable argument, made repeatedly by owners, that the original S30 was the better value for deep-sky work specifically. That argument is now mostly academic, since the S30 has been discontinued.
