The scope that gets recommended for good reason

The Evostar 72ED turns up in more "first serious telescope" recommendations than almost anything else, and the reasons are unglamorous. It is 72mm of aperture at 420mm focal length, f/5.8, using an air-spaced doublet with an ED element. Tube weight is around two kilograms.

That combination lands in a useful place. 420mm frames most large nebulae comfortably on a small sensor. f/5.8 is fast enough to gather signal without demanding the tolerances a faster system does. Two kilograms sits within the comfortable range of even modest mounts, which means the mount you already own can probably carry it.

Doublet against triplet

A doublet uses two elements where a triplet uses three. Fewer elements means less glass, less weight, lower cost, and slightly less complete colour correction. In practice a good ED doublet at f/5.8 controls false colour well enough that it is not the limiting factor in a deep-sky image. Bright stars are where any residual shows first.

The saving is real and worth being honest about: the money not spent on a third element is money available for the mount, which will affect your images more.

What you will need on top

This is the part that catches people out. For imaging you will want a field flattener, because a fast doublet does not deliver a flat field to the corners of a sensor on its own. Stars towards the edges will elongate without one. The flattener is an additional purchase and also changes the backfocus arithmetic.

The supplied focuser is a 2 inch dual-speed Crayford with 10:1 fine focus, which is genuinely good at this price and takes a motorised focuser without complaint.

The other job it does

At 420mm and two kilograms it makes a capable guidescope, and a genuinely pleasant grab-and-go visual instrument. A scope that has three uses is easier to justify than one that has one.

Who it suits

Anyone starting deep-sky imaging who wants a telescope that will not become the weak link, and anyone who needs a second short-focal-length instrument. If you already own something in the 400mm to 500mm range, the overlap is substantial and there is little reason to add it.
